CORE SCIENTIFIC QUESTION

Your smartwatch can tell you when your heart rate rises—but can it tell you why, whether it matters, and what to do next?

How can we reliably infer changing human physiological states from heterogeneous, imperfect observations—and determine when those changes matter and what evidence to acquire next?

THE MODEL

From longitudinal observations to personalized models

Every measurement reflects two processes that a personalized model must tell apart: how each person’s physiological state evolves under dynamics of their own, and how sensors turn that state into data.

STATE-SPACE VIEW · INDIVIDUAL i OVER TIME

Hidden states evolve under each person’s own dynamics; every sensor reading is a noisy, condition-dependent view of the current state.

THREE THRUSTS

Three questions, one loop

Recover what is hidden. Tell what a change means. Decide what to measure next.

THRUST I

Observability

What can we reliably recover?

Establish which physiological states are identifiable from integrated electrical, mechanical, biochemical, and ambient measurements.

x1 ≠ x2 ⇒ p(y | x1) ≠ p(y | x2)
identifiable: different states leave different evidence
THRUST II

Interpretability

When does a change actually matter?

Model individual physiological dynamics, contextual influences, measurement processes, and uncertainty to distinguish genuine state changes from observational artifacts.

p(Δxi,t ≠ 0 | yi,1:t, c, d)
is the change genuine, given context c and measurement process d?
THRUST III

Actionability

What evidence should we acquire next?

Develop uncertainty-aware AI that selects informative, low-burden observations and updates personalized physiological models.

a* = argmaxa I(xi,t; ya) − λ·cost(a)
trade the information gained against measurement burden
