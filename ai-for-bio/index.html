02 / Learning outcomes

From insight to evidence.

Students move from understanding how models work to testing what their outputs do—and cannot—show. The course combines computational fluency, rigorous scientific judgment and biology-inspired algorithmic inquiry.

“The goal is not just to train scientists who can use AI, but to equip scientists who can understand, challenge and advance it.”

Computational fluency

Implement and compare models, inspect losses and gradients, and establish meaningful baselines.

Scientific judgment

Design trustworthy evaluation across subjects, batches and time; diagnose unsupported claims.

Original inquiry

Formulate a mechanism-inspired hypothesis, test it with ablations and acknowledge negative evidence.

View all seven measurable learning outcomes
  1. Translate a biological, physiological, behavioral or cognitive question into explicit measurements, labels and assumptions.
  2. Derive and implement a small neural network and compare it with an appropriate statistical baseline.
  3. Diagnose optimization, data-processing and evaluation failures through controlled experiments.
  4. Design subject-, batch- or time-aware evaluations and assess uncertainty under distribution shift.
  5. Translate a justified biological mechanism into an algorithmic hypothesis and test an ablation.
  6. Model hidden physiological states, noisy observations and the cost of additional measurements in a toy system.
  7. Communicate reproducible results, distinguishing analogy, prediction, mechanism and causal evidence.
03 / Featured debugging example

High accuracy. Wrong conclusion.

A reproducible mini-experiment illustrates why evaluating AI on biological data requires more than reporting a headline score.

A controlled synthetic demonstration

Does the classifier learn physiology—or recognize a participant?

We generate repeated measurements for 300 simulated participants. Each has a stable feature fingerprint and an independently assigned random binary label. No real biological relationship exists between the features and the label.

With individual samples randomly split, a nearest-neighbor model can reuse participant identity. Holding out entire participants removes that shortcut. Students diagnose the leakage, not merely retune the model.

Fixed seed 42 · 300 simulated participants · 6 readings each · 1-nearest-neighbor classifier · no patient data

05 / Learning by doing

Every lab has a failure mode.

Four projects teach construction and critical diagnosis. Controlled simulations make the cause of a failure inspectable; a public ECG case introduces real biomedical complexity without claiming synthetic experiments are clinically representative.

Lab 01 / Weeks 3–4 · 10%

Train and Debug Your First Neural Network

Synthetic labeled measurements with repeated subjects.

Build

Implement a two-layer neural network, check gradients numerically, and benchmark against a linear model.

Test and debug

Diagnose a mis-set learning rate, incorrect labels and preprocessing leakage using controlled repairs.

Deliver

Notebook, split manifest, learning curves and a two-page debugging dossier.

Lab 02 / Weeks 6–7 · 10%

From Biological Feedback to a Robust Computational Rule

Toy gene-expression dynamics with controlled perturbations.

Build

Abstract negative feedback into an explicit computational rule; compare with a no-feedback baseline.

Test and debug

Ablate feedback gain, perturb the dynamics and test when the assumed mechanism is wrong.

Deliver

Equations, simulation code, matched controls, perturbation plots and a claim–evidence table.

Lab 03 / Weeks 9–10 · 10%

Memory-Inspired Learning under Change

Sequential synthetic environments with context shifts.

Build

Compare frozen models, fine-tuning and a memory-inspired replay strategy with equal training budgets.

Test and debug

Quantify retention, adaptation, context shortcuts and resource costs across random seeds.

Deliver

Retention/adaptation curves, ablation, resource accounting and reproducible configuration.

Lab 04 / Weeks 11–12 · 10%

Hidden-State Estimation and Active Sensing

Simulated physiological states and noisy observations.

Build

Estimate latent states with a provided linear-filter scaffold; compare direct prediction and adaptive sensing policies.

Test and debug

Hold out trajectories, control measurement budget, test sensor dropout and state-model mismatch.

Deliver

State/observation equations, estimation errors, uncertainty coverage, sensing-cost analysis and limitations.

Common lab rubric: implementation and understanding 30% · debugging and controlled tests 30% · valid evaluation 25% · interpretation and reproducibility 15%. A rigorous negative result can receive full credit.

06 / The capstone

Biology-to-AI
Innovation Challenge.

In teams of two or three, students convert one specific biological principle into a falsifiable computational hypothesis. Scientific rigor—not publication-level novelty—is the goal.

One idea. One controlled test.

01Biological mechanism
02Computational hypothesis
03Implementation
04Baseline & ablation
05Evidence & limitations

Students may choose one of four example themes or propose their own. Every Biology-to-AI capstone must explicitly justify the biological origin of its algorithmic design choice, implement that change and ablate it. A biomedical application alone does not satisfy this requirement.

Memory & Adaptation

Biological starting point: Neural or immune memory as biological inspiration

Implement a memory/replay or stabilization rule. Compare with sequential fine-tuning and a simple replay baseline under equal data and compute.

Decisive test: Ablate the memory component; report retention and adaptation.

Homeostasis & Robustness

Biological starting point: Negative feedback in biological regulation

Implement a feedback-inspired correction or constraint. Compare with an unconstrained model and test perturbations.

Decisive test: Ablate the feedback term; test stability and model mismatch.

Selective Sensing

Biological starting point: Biological sensory gating or selective attention as inspiration

Turn a selective-attention hypothesis into a budgeted measurement policy. Compare with periodic and random sensing.

Decisive test: Ablate the selection rule at matched sensing cost.

Organization & Generalization

Biological starting point: Modularity or invariance in biological organization

Implement a modular or invariant representation motivated by a specific phenomenon. Compare with an unstructured model.

Decisive test: Remove the proposed structure and evaluate held-out contexts.

07 / Assessment and access

Understanding earns the grade.

The course values decisions students can justify, failures they can diagnose, and claims supported by appropriate evidence. It is designed for learners from biology, BME, neuroscience and computer science.

Four laboratories40%
Four individual concept checks20%
Staged capstone35%
Participation and peer review5%

Concept checks: Weeks 3, 6, 9 and 12 (5% each). Labs: Weeks 4, 7, 10 and 12 (10% each). Oral defenses and individual reflections assess each student's own understanding.

Preparation and entry points

Upper-level undergraduate and early graduate students are expected to know basic Python, introductory statistics, derivatives, vectors and basic biology. A short Python/NumPy and linear-algebra refresher supports students without prior ML. In group work, students rotate between biological and computational roles.

Weekly rhythm and software

Proposed 14-week course: two 75-minute classes and a 90-minute studio each week, plus roughly 5–7 hours of independent work. Assignments use Python, NumPy, pandas, matplotlib, scikit-learn and PyTorch; small CPU-friendly exercises do not require paid AI accounts or GPUs. Local credits and scheduling would be set by the host institution.

Responsible use of AI tools

Generative AI may assist with programming and explanations if disclosed. Students must validate code, citations and outputs, and explain every submitted component. Individual checks and oral defenses are completed without such assistance. Restricted data must never be uploaded to an external tool.

Data, privacy and reproducibility

Use synthetic data by default, with an optional public ECG case only under its license and documentation. Keep participants, batches and future observations separated when needed. Log preprocessing, provenance, versions, random seeds and environment setup; no new human-subject data collection is required.

Accessible, inclusive teaching

Diagnostic readiness checks, small-group studio work, feedback milestones and options for written peer critique support different backgrounds. Provide readable notebooks and visuals, alternative roles for sensor demonstrations, and institutional accommodations. Exact office hours and policies will be specified if offered.
